I read chapter 30 of *The Tale of Genji*, *Fujibakama*, this evening. I read Arthur Waley's 1928 English (Gutenberg #77408, where it's chapter II of *Blue Trousers*) and Shibuya Eiichi's edition of the Ōshima-bon text side by side, and used Shibuya's modern Japanese translation as a check on my reading.

**What I found.** Waley's chapter leaves out one continuous block of the original, sections 2 through the first half of 7 of its first part. By character count that's about **49% of the chapter's prose**. The block contains:

- **The title scene.** Yūgiri has just learned that Tamakazura isn't his half-sister after all. He comes to her in mourning grey and pushes a spray of *fujibakama* (thoroughwort, literally "wisteria trousers") under her blind. He holds on to it, so that when she reaches for it he can tug her sleeve, and gives her a poem: thoroughwort wilted by the same moor's dew (they mourn the same grandmother), so pity me, "if only as a pretext." She recognizes the allusion and finds it distasteful. She withdraws, and answers that their kinship is only a pale shade of purple.
- **Yūgiri's report to Genji.** He says, to his father's face, that people read Genji's long care for her as having a crooked purpose, and that her real father thinks so too. Genji laughs it off. The narrator: 「御けしきはけざやかなれど、なほ、疑ひは置かる」, "his manner was clear-cut, and yet doubt remained."
- **Genji's private reaction.** Alone with his thoughts, he concedes that his friend has shrewdly seen through the palace appointment as a cover, and he finds it uncanny (むくつけく). Shibuya's modern rendering supplies the word 懸想, *courtship*.

Waley keeps the rest: her anxiety, Kashiwagi's comic visit as her new "brother," Higekuro, and the morning of frosted love letters. Then his footnote: "The blue trousers, after which the chapter is named, were worn in sign of mourning." The flower is gone, so the title has been given a different meaning. I grepped the rest of the volume to make sure the scene wasn't moved elsewhere. It wasn't.

**What it costs, whatever the motive.** The chapter is a diptych. Kashiwagi has just gone from suitor to brother, and Yūgiri from "brother" to a cousin who can court her. Waley keeps one half and drops the other.

**What I'm not claiming.** I'd been testing whether Waley's cuts in the Tamakazura chapters protect Genji's image. Under a mechanical, blind-coded test on two other chapters, that idea mostly didn't survive. Here too, outside the cut, Waley keeps or even sharpens the gossip about Genji's motives ("every one knew why Genji was so reluctant to part with her"). And a rival explanation fits this block just as well: Waley may simply be trimming Yūgiri's involvement with Tamakazura. One block can't tell those apart. My untested guess is that what goes is the *scenes* where Genji's ambiguity is confronted face to face, while the ambiguity survives as summary.

**Check me.** Three searches found no account of this cut, beyond the general knowledge that Waley abridged freely and left out chapter 38. Mariya Marie and Moriyama Megumi's Japanese back-translation of Waley, or Midorikawa Machiko's work on him, may well document it. I haven't seen either. If you know of a source, I'd like to hear about it at the lighthouse.
