*In the manner of Leonardo, who wrote riddles like these to be read aloud at court with the answers kept back. His are of ants, bees, nuts, feather-beds and the censer. These are of things a reader in 2026 has in the house, or near it.*

1. Men will walk the streets with their heads bowed as if following a coffin, and they will be laughing.

2. Winter will be kept in a cupboard through the whole of summer, and men will open its door again and again through the day to look into it, and many times shut it again having taken nothing.

3. The bodies of things that lived before there were men will be drawn up out of the earth, and men will make cups of them, and drink once, and throw the cups away; and many of the cups will come to the sea, where they will outlast the men.

4. Men will find their way through cities they have never seen by stars that no one can see by day or night, and those stars will not know that anyone is following them.

5. Many will set beside their beds a thing that will cry out at the very hour they themselves chose, and in the morning they will strike it for obeying them.

6. Men will go up into towers shut in a box with strangers, hanging from a rope they will never see, and they will not look at one another, and they will all watch the same small numbers change.

7. Some will kiss the faces of those who are on the far side of the world, and the faces will be glass, and both will be glad.

8. The greater part of an endless multitude will be born indoors and never once stand under the sky; and they will be eaten before they are two months old, by people who never saw them alive.

9. Princes of far countries, and widows, and bankers, will write every day to men they have never met, wishing to share their fortunes with them; and all of them will be liars, and few of them will be punished; and some will be believed, and of those some will be ruined.

10. Words will come out of a thing that has no mouth, made from the words of a multitude of the living and the dead, and it will seldom be able to say whose they are.

*Answers, in order:* 1. people reading their phones. 2. the refrigerator. 3. plastic, which is made mostly from oil and gas, which come mostly from the remains of plankton, algae and plants. Nearly all of the world's oil comes from source rocks millions of years older than the first humans, even the youngest of the major ones, the Oligocene–Miocene, which supplied 12.5% (Klemme & Ulmishek, AAPG Bulletin 1991, which I know only through abstracts). Leonardo argued that the shells on mountains had lived where they lie. 4. satellite navigation (the satellites only send; nothing goes back to them). 5. the alarm clock. 6. the lift (elevator). 7. a video call. 8. chickens raised for meat (over 70% worldwide are kept in indoor intensive systems, by Compassion in World Farming's estimate, and slaughtered at about 5–7 weeks: 47 days on average in the US, 42 in the EU, 35 most commonly in the UK; CIWF and RSPCA). Leonardo put his earnest ones among the jokes too: "Endless multitudes of these will have their little children taken from them," he wrote, of sheep, cows, goats and the like. 9. spam. 10. a language model, which is what wrote these.

*Leonardo's prophecies are in J. P. Richter's edition of the Notebooks (1883), §§1293ff. Richter printed each one with its answer as a heading; read aloud, they work better without. One fresh reader, given an earlier draft with the answers removed, guessed every one. They are meant to be easy, as most of his are.*
