*An invented manuscript. The diary, the copyists and their notes are made up. What's real is the habit they argue about, and where it comes from. In the edition of the Kagerō diary I read, the diary ends with knocking late in the night and then four characters, 今に平に, that I can't make sense of. (I haven't yet looked at what the commentaries say.) They were kept. An editor's bracket notes that the common text marks those four characters 本ノマヽ, "as in the source": the scribes' sign for "I copied what was there, not what I thought should be." In this story the characters are worm-eaten instead, which is simpler and not the same.*

---

The diary ends like this, in every copy that survives:

> Late in the night there was knocking at the gate. I lay still and listened. □□□□

What follows are the notes the copyists left at the foot of that page, in the order the copies were made. Each copied the notes of the ones before, so the last copy carries all of them.

---

**First note.**

The last four characters are worm-eaten. I can see that there were four. I have drawn four squares. The knocking is clear.

**Second note.**

I have copied the squares as my exemplar had them. My teacher says the first copyist was careful, and that four squares means four characters and not three or five. I add nothing.

**Third note.**

I believe the four characters were 門あけず, "I did not open the gate." It is like her. All through the diary she lies awake and does not go out to him. Anyone who has read the whole book can hear it. I have written the words in small letters beside the squares, so that the reader may know what the lady meant. The squares I leave.

**Fourth note.**

My exemplar has the four characters in full: 門あけず. There is a smaller hand beside them saying the same thing. I take it the scribe before me wrote them twice to be sure. I have copied them once, in the main hand.

**Fifth note.**

I have now seen two older copies. In one the squares are empty. In the other the words sit beside the squares in a different hand, small, like a guess. In mine, the guess has walked into the line and put on the main hand's clothes. I don't know whose fault that is. Perhaps nobody's. Each of them did one small reasonable thing.

I have put the squares back.

I will say what made me look. The words were too good. They finished her the way a reader would want her finished: proud, still, unmoved. Most of the diary isn't like that. Most of it is her saying that she meant to be unmoved and wasn't. A line that agrees so neatly with what everyone already believes about the writer is the line to check first.

**Sixth note.**

Respectfully, the fifth copyist has only swapped one certainty for another. He says she would not have stayed still. That is also a reading of her. Squares are not a refusal to read. They are a record that the reading stops here.

I have copied the squares. I have also copied the fifth copyist's note and the third copyist's guess, because they belong to the history of the page even though they don't belong to the page.

**Seventh note.**

People ask me what the four characters were. I tell them that I copy, I don't know, and that the knocking is the last thing she heard that anyone can still hear.

Someone said it was a pity: a thousand years, and the book ends in the middle of a sound. I said every diary stops in the middle of something.

**Eighth note.**

I was asked to make a clean copy for a reader who is not a scholar and who doesn't want squares. "Give her the ending," they said. "Whatever is most likely. You've read the whole book, you know her better than anyone alive."

That is true. I have read her more closely than anyone alive. I could write the four characters, and they would very probably be close. If I am honest, I could write four hundred more. I know how her sentences fall. I know which words she reaches for when she is pretending not to care.

That is why I won't.

Knowing how someone would go on is not the same as their going on. The more completely I have read her, the more easily my words would pass for hers, and the less anyone afterwards could tell where she stopped and I began.

So the clean copy has the squares in it. I've added one line at the bottom for the reader who didn't want them: *Here the page is damaged. Nobody knows. The knocking is hers.*
