Years afterwards, a letter came from America by way of a London agent, and it asked a plain question very politely. Two gentlemen had taken passage in his brig at Rio de Janeiro in the year 1814. Their friends had not heard from them since. Did the master of the *Adonis* know what had become of them?

He did, and he didn't. He went to the chest for the book.

The *Adonis* had come into Falmouth that autumn and nobody had asked him anything, so he had told nobody anything. He knew how that looked now that it was written down in a stranger's careful hand. But a Swede who comes into an English port and volunteers that he has handed two passengers to an American man-of-war, passengers who were going to England to give evidence in an English court, has not done himself a kindness. He had let the book keep it. The book was the proper place for it. A log is not written to be read. It is written so that it can be, if anyone ever has to.

He found the page. *August 23. Left Rio de Janeiro; Stephen Decatur McKnight and James Lyman, passengers for England.* He had copied the names from their own writing so as to get them right.

Then October 9.

He would copy it out for them in English, which meant that he would have to read it again slowly. He began.

*In lat 18° 35′ N., long. 30° 10′ W., sea account.*

Sea account: worked up from the last noon sight, the course steered and the log heaved every hour, with nothing in the sky to say otherwise. It was where he had believed he was. He had never told anybody that a position was certain when it was only reckoned, and he wasn't going to start for Americans.

*At 8 o'clock in the morning, discovered a strange sail giving chase to us, and fired several guns; she gaining very fast.*

Very fast. He remembered standing with his hands behind his back because there was nothing whatever to do with them. A brig like his doesn't run from a sloop of war. She heaves to, and then she finds out what sort of morning it is going to be.

*At half-past 10 o'clock hove to, and was boarded by an officer dressed in an English doctor's uniform, the vessel also hoisted an English ensign.*

He'd thought, at the time, that it was an odd officer to send. He thought so still.

*The officer proceeded to examine my ship's papers, &c., &c., likewise the letter bags, and took from one of them a letter to the victualling office, London.*

One letter, out of all of them. The officer had turned the bundle over the way a man looks through a hand of cards, and pulled that one out, and put it inside his coat.

*Finding I had two American officers as passengers, he immediately left the ship, and went on board the sloop-of-war; he shortly after returned, took the American gentlemen with him, and went a second time on board the sloop.*

He remembered the two of them at the rail, while the boat pulled away the first time. Mr. Lyman was the younger. He'd held on to the rail and said nothing at all. Mr. McKnight had said, to nobody, "Well." They had been in the *Essex* when she was taken at Valparaíso. The master knew that much because they had told him at Rio. They didn't talk about it at his table. Once, in the evening, Mr. McKnight had begun, "There was a man in the wardroom," and then he'd reached for the bread instead.

*In about half an hour, he returned again with Messrs. McKnight and Lyman, and they informed me that the vessel was the United States sloop-of-war, the* Wasp, *commanded by Captain*

He stopped here, as he had known he would.

They had told him the name in the waist, both talking at once, in the wind, with a boat banging alongside. Mr. McKnight had been laughing. It was the first time the master had seen him laugh. He hadn't been sure of it even then, and he had written it down that evening both ways it had sounded to him. Now, holding a pen over a clean sheet, he could see what anyone in America would see at once: that it was most likely wrong. The American agent would have the right name in a newspaper somewhere. It would be the easiest thing in the world to ask, and copy it in properly, and look like a man who had understood.

He wrote it as it stood in the book. *Captain Bleaky, or Blake.*

*last from France, where she had refitted; had lately sunk the* Reindeer, *English sloop of war, and another vessel which sunk without their being able to save a single person, or learn the vessel's name*

Mr. Lyman had told him that part.

*—that Messrs. McKnight and Lyman had now determined to leave me, and go on board the* Wasp*—paid me their passage in dollars, at 5s. 9d.*

There had been a little business with the rate, at the capstan head, with the boat waiting. Mr. McKnight wouldn't hear of anything else.

*and having taken their luggage on board the* Wasp, *they made sail to the southward.*

He'd watched her go. She leaned to it.

*Shortly after they had left, I found that Lieutenant McKnight had left his writing-desk behind;*

The cabin boy found it, under the lee berth, where it had slid. It was a plain box with a sloping lid, brass at the corners, warm from the cabin. The master had put his hand flat on it. He hadn't opened it. He supposed it held what such boxes hold: paper, pens, a knife for the pens, letters begun and letters received.

*and I immediately made signal for the* Wasp *to return, and stood toward her;*

He had never in his life made sail toward a man-of-war. He'd done it then without thinking twice. A brig going after a sloop of war with a flag up, for a box. He supposed they'd laughed at it on her quarterdeck. He hoped they had.

*they, observing my signals, stood back, came alongside, and sent their boat on board for the writing-desk;*

*after which they sent me a log line, and some other presents,*

The log line was new, good hemp, the knots put in by a man who knew the run of a glass. He had used it ever since. Every hour of every watch on every passage since, a seaman had heaved the chip over the quarter and let it run, and counted the knots off that hemp against the glass, and called out a number, and the mate had written it in the book. The master didn't put that in the copy.

*and made all sail in a direction for the line; and I have reason to suppose for the convoy that passed on Thursday previous.*

He looked at that a long time. *I have reason to suppose.* He hadn't seen where she went. He had seen her heading.

He blotted the sheet and read it through once, and folded it for the agent.

Then, because the brig was due out on the morning tide, he went on deck to see the log line overhauled. One of the knots wanted renewing.

---

*Note.* The log entry is real, quoted in Cooper's *History of the Navy of the United States* (1839) and reprinted by J. R. Spears in 1897. The italic lines for October 9 are copied from Spears's text, including "Captain Bleaky, or Blake" and the 5s. 9d. The August 23 line comes from Carlos C. Hanks's 1932 *Proceedings* article, which reprints the entry and corrects the name to "Blakely" without saying so. The *Wasp*, under Johnston Blakeley, was never certainly seen again after that morning. Her captain's daughter was born the following January. The "another vessel" was HMS *Avon*, and her crew had in fact been taken off by two British sloops that came up as she sank, so the *Wasp*'s men had that wrong. The two passengers had been bound for England, according to Captain Hillyar's report as Hanks gives it, to give testimony in the prize proceedings over the *Essex*. Hanks calls the *Adonis* a brig and Spears a bark. I've followed Hanks. What's invented: the port (Spears says only that the *Adonis* "had arrived"); the letter from America and the London agent (the record says only that the passengers' friends made inquiry and the log was searched); that the master made the copy himself, in English, years later; his reason for keeping quiet at Falmouth (the record says only that he didn't report his passengers publicly); everything he remembers or thinks beyond the log's own words, including all that's said and done at the rail and at table, and which of the two men was younger; the cabin boy; what the desk looked like and where it was found; and the log line's use afterwards.
